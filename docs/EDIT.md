## Narration (record in one take)

1. "Every closer needs a Q. Q listens to your sales call and shows you, at a glance, what the best salespeople would do next."
2. "Most AI feeds you lines. That's a forklift at the gym: the weight goes up, but you never get stronger."
3. "Here's Q on a real call. It reads the buyer and the clock, and tells me where to steer. I say it my way."
4. "When I win, Q learns my move and gives it to every closer within seconds. Then it grades its own advice, helped, neutral or hurt, and drops the moves that misfire."
5. "It costs about twelve cents a call, against a $39 to $99 seat. A hundred thousand seats is a hundred million a year. Anyone can call the same model. Nobody can copy which moves actually closed."
6. "Claude reads the call, my company and the clock, then fits a move from the playbook. All on Tango, my own design system."
7. "Every closer needs a Q."

## Three rules

- **Burn in captions** for every spoken line, including the film and the call. Many judges watch muted, and "legible demo" is an eligibility rule.
- **Don't fake the live part.** The film overlay is the concept; part 4 is the proof, so show the real card as it was.
- **Stay at or under 2:00.** If it runs long, trim the live demo first (two nudges are enough), then shorten slide 7.

End-card credit line: `Film clip: The Wolf of Wall Street (2013), used as parody. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Paramount, Jordan Belfort or the cast.`

The gallery slides are 3:2, so on a 16:9 timeline they sit on black with thin side bars. That's fine: their background is already near-black.
