## The idea

**The hook:** Jordan Belfort's first call at the Investors Center, straight from the film. A Q card floats top right. Each time the buyer talks, the card flashes a nudge and Jordan makes exactly that move. Once, he goes off the card with a move of his own; the deal closes, and Q learns his move. The coworker beside him (John, face-swapped) stares: "How'd you do that?" The answer is the title: "Every closer needs a Q."

**Then the proof:** a real Q on a real Google Meet call. John acts on live nudges, improvises a move Q has never seen, wins, and Q hands that move to another seller in seconds, then grades its own nudges.

**Messaging, in this order:** the hero line "Every closer needs a Q." (the Bond angle: Q hands the agent the right gadget at the right moment), then the tagline "your cue to close", then the plain-English line the first time we explain the product: "Q listens to your sales call and shows you, at a glance, what the best salespeople would do next.", followed by the wow: every win teaches it something new. For the business beat, the category is **closing intelligence**: the moves that are winning right now, getting sharper with every deal.

**Tone:** the film brings the swagger. Everything after it is calm and certain: a company that has shipped, a real product on a real call, real numbers.

## Production notes

### The film clip

- Get the Investors Center scene from a source you're allowed to view, and keep only the pieces in the hook tables plus a few listening shots: under 30 seconds of film from a scene of about 2 minutes. Short, transformed, non-commercial and credited is the safest shape for parody; it isn't a guarantee.
- Skip the scene's first line (the crude offer before Jordan dials). Bleep the one swear in the coworker's line and caption it `[bleep]`.
- The end card's small line credits the film: `Film clip: The Wolf of Wall Street (2013), used as parody. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Paramount, Jordan Belfort or the cast.`

### The face swap (Higgsfield)

- Swap only John's face, only onto the coworker beside Jordan who stares at him in disbelief. Never touch Jordan's face or anyone else's.
- Upload just his shots (2 to 3 seconds each) and a sharp, front-lit photo of John at a similar angle. Render at the clip's resolution and drop it back on the timeline in place.
- If the "How'd you do that?" line comes from a different man than the one who stares, swap the one who stares and keep the line on the audio.
- No AI voice clone of anyone. The coworker's line keeps the film's audio.

### Building the Q card overlay

The overlay sits top right, about a fifth of the frame's width (the card is 360 px wide on a laptop screen), in the same spot for the whole hook. It's the only scripted card text in the video; the live demo's card is never edited.

**Option A, the real card (recommended):** the stand-in Meet page `extension/dev/meet.html` renders the real card. The snippet below answers the card's requests with the three beats, so the real card does the rest: `Hearing John…`, `Thinking…`, the fade, the credit line, the moment label, the buyer chip and the toast. **Untested; try it once before 8:30.** If anything misbehaves, use option B.

1. Serve the extension folder locally (for example `python3 -m http.server 8000 --directory extension` from the repo, then open `http://localhost:8000/dev/meet.html` in Chrome). Don't press "Play a gym call".
2. Open DevTools, paste the snippet once (reload the page to start over), and wait 3 seconds.
3. Start the screen recording (the card only), then type, one at a time, pausing between beats:
   - `buyer(1, "Yeah? Who's this? I'm kind of busy.")`
   - `jordan("If you have sixty seconds I'd like to share the idea with you")` (optional: lights `Hearing you…`)
   - `buyer(2, "Penny stocks, though? Sounds risky.")`
   - `buyer(3, "Alright. I could do four thousand.")`
   - `learned()` (the toast lands within about 3 seconds)
4. Crop to the card with a rounded-corner mask; crop the toast pill on its own for 0:28. Retime in the edit so each nudge lands about a second after its caption. Shift+C shows the stand-in Meet captions, if you'd rather record the buyer captions than build them.
