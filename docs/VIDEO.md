> **Risk note:** Jordan Belfort appears by name as a character played by a friend in a non-commercial concept skit; nothing in the video says or implies he endorses or uses Q, and there is no AI voice or face clone of him. A real movie or seminar clip can get muted or flagged by YouTube's Content ID, so the reenactment below (or a clip of a few seconds) is the safer choice; John decides.

## The idea

**Take 1:** Jordan Belfort slides a pen across the table, says "Sell me this pen", and John freezes while a stopwatch counts the silence. **Take 2:** same pen, same guy, now on Google Meet with Q; John acts on Q's nudges through the objections, then flips the famous test back on Belfort to close him. Q learns that move, hands it to every other seller, and grades its own nudges after the call.

**Messaging, in this order:** the hero line "Every closer needs a Q." (the Bond angle: Q hands the agent the right gadget at the right moment), then the tagline "your cue to close", then the plain-English line the first time we explain the product: "Q listens to your sales call and shows you, at a glance, what the best salespeople would do next.", followed by the wow: every win teaches it something new. For the business beat, the category is **closing intelligence**: the moves that are winning right now, getting sharper with every deal.

Tone: deadpan mockumentary. Nobody mugs for the camera, nobody shouts. The stopwatch is the joke, and it pays off at the end with real numbers.

## Production notes

- Only real numbers on screen: the freeze time, the time to the first nudge and the toast-to-toast time all come from your footage.
- **The verdicts are real. Never stage, fake or edit them.** John acts on each nudge in his own words because that's how Q is meant to be used, not to game the review. If a take ends with most nudges ignored (a fumbled line, a missed nudge), reshoot the call rather than editing around the report.
- Match the site: "The starter playbook (its moves, names and records) is sample data for this demo. Moves learned on live calls are real." So never say the starter moves came from real closers; say "moves" or "the playbook". The credits on screen (Ana · Los Angeles, Maya · Chicago) are sample names; the move you teach Q on camera is the real thing.
- Cost is always "on our standard model": the demo runs Claude Opus 5.5 in fast mode for speed, and the twelve-cent figure is our standard model's measured cost.
- Speed is "about a second". Never claim sub-second.
- Primary descriptor is closing intelligence, not "live closing coach", and never "copilot".
- Never edit or fake a nudge. If the card shows something weak, keep the take honest or reshoot the beat.
