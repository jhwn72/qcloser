# Q: 2-minute video script

> **Risk note:** Jordan Belfort appears by name as a character played by a friend in a non-commercial concept skit; nothing in the video says or implies he endorses or uses Q, and there is no AI voice or face clone of him. A real movie or seminar clip can get muted or flagged by YouTube's Content ID, so the reenactment below (or a clip of a few seconds) is the safer choice; John decides.

## The idea

**Take 1:** Jordan Belfort slides a pen across the table, says "Sell me this pen", and John freezes while a stopwatch counts the silence. **Take 2:** same pen, same guy, now on Google Meet with Q; Q gets John through the objections, John flips the famous test back on Belfort to close him, and Q learns that move and hands it to every other seller.

**Messaging, in this order:** the hero line "Every closer needs a Q." (the Bond angle: Q hands the agent the right gadget at the right moment), then the tagline "your cue to close", then the plain-English line the first time we explain the product: "Q listens to your sales call and shows you what the best salespeople would do next, in five words", followed by the wow: every win teaches it something new. For the business beat, the category is **closing intelligence**: the moves that are winning right now, getting sharper with every deal.

Tone: deadpan mockumentary. Nobody mugs for the camera, nobody shouts. The stopwatch is the joke, and it pays off at the end with real numbers.

## Cast and gear

- **John Wang:** himself. Seller and narrator.
- **Jordan Belfort:** played by one helper. Suit jacket, slicked-back hair, a calm, bored stare. Plays it flat. In Take 2 he's the buyer on the Meet call, on the phone, from another room.
- **Gear:** the laptop (Chrome with Q, Google Meet, a screen recorder), the phone (camera for Take 1, then it joins the Meet as the buyer), a **black** pen (the joke needs it black), a table, a plain wall.

## Shot list, in filming order (about 30 minutes)

Do the setup in [DEMO_SCRIPT.md](DEMO_SCRIPT.md#before-you-film) first (API credits, database reset, popup, second Chrome profile, the rehearsal that ends Lost). That isn't counted below.

| # | Shot | Device | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Take 1, wide. 2 or 3 takes. | Phone on a tripod or a stack of books, landscape | 5 min |
| 2 | Take 1, close on John (pick up, "So. This is a pen", the clicks). Skip if short on time and punch in during the edit. | Phone | 3 min |
| 3 | Stinger: Belfort alone, "It is a nice pen." | Phone | 1 min |
| 4 | Helper takes the phone to another room and joins the Meet. John joins on the laptop about 4.5 minutes before the first line. Recorder test (see below) in that wait. | Both | 5 min |
| 5 | The live call, start to finish, per DEMO_SCRIPT.md. Keep recording through End call and Won. | Laptop screen recording | 4 min |
| 6 | Bring up the second Chrome window, catch the toast. Then /playbook: the new move, the feed, a retired move. | Laptop screen recording | 3 min |
| 7 | Landing page hero (title shot). | Laptop screen recording | 1 min |
| 8 | Problem B-roll: John glancing at a text wall; Meet's "You left the meeting" screen. | Phone + laptop | 2 min |
| 9 | Narration, all lines in one go, quiet room, phone voice memo a hand's width from your mouth. | Phone | 4 min |

If the call needs a second take, reset at /admin first (Q won't relearn a move that's already in the playbook), and remember the reset also clears the rehearsal's loss lesson.

**Recording the call's audio:** QuickTime records only the mic, so Belfort's voice from Meet can go missing. Use a recorder that also captures computer audio (for example OBS, or Loom with computer audio on) and play back a 20-second test before the real take. Wear earbuds on the laptop so Belfort's voice doesn't leak into your mic and your captions.

**Edit:** CapCut or iMovie. Burned-in captions, chapter labels always in the same corner, the stopwatch as plain monospace text, room tone instead of music (deadpan dies under music). Export 1080p. Upload to YouTube as unlisted (or Loom) and check the length is 2:00 or less.

## If it runs long

Cut in this order: the 1:00 stall line (keep the clock as on-screen text), the "J. B." exchange, the retired-move pass on the playbook, the stinger, then the stopwatch on the second-card toast.

## Solo version

- **A remote friend is the buyer:** they join the Meet from their own phone or laptop as Belfort. Shoot Take 1 as a Meet call too: Q switched off in the popup, the pen line, John freezes. Switch Q on for Take 2 (test the toggle once first). Keep the lower third.
- **Truly alone:** type the buyer lines into the card with Shift+B and say so on screen (`Buyer lines typed for this take`). It's a weaker demo, so use it only if nobody can join.

## Production notes

- Only real numbers on screen: the freeze time, the time to the first nudge and the toast-to-toast time all come from your footage.
- Match the site: "The starter playbook (its moves, names and records) is sample data for this demo. Moves learned on live calls are real." So never say the starter moves came from real closers; say "moves" or "the playbook". The credits on screen (Ana · Los Angeles, Maya · Chicago) are sample names; the move you teach Q on camera is the real thing.
- Primary descriptor is closing intelligence, not "live closing coach", and never "copilot".
- Never edit or fake a nudge. If the card shows something weak, keep the take honest or reshoot the beat.
